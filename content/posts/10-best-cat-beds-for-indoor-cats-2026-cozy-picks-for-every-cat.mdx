## How to Choose the Best Cat Bed for Your Indoor Cat

A good cat bed should offer more than an attractive appearance. It should provide a comfortable resting space, suit your cat's sleeping habits, and be practical enough for everyday use.

Here are seven important things to consider before purchasing.

### 1. Understand Your Cat's Sleeping Position

Start by observing how your cat normally sleeps.

You might notice that your cat curls up into a tiny ball, stretches out across the sofa, or hides under blankets. These habits can help you choose a suitable bed.

- **Cats that curl up:** Look for round beds, donut beds, or designs with raised sides.
- **Cats that stretch out:** Consider larger cushions or open sleeping surfaces.
- **Cats that enjoy hiding:** An enclosed or cave-style bed may provide a more private resting space.
- **Cats that like elevated positions:** Consider a securely installed elevated resting option.

Don't assume every cat will enjoy the same design. Your cat's existing sleeping habits are the best starting point.

### 2. Choose Comfortable and Cat-Friendly Materials

The material affects how a cat bed feels, how much warmth it retains, and how easy it is to clean.

Here are some common materials worth considering.

![Comparison of plush, fleece, cotton, polyester, and memory foam materials used in cat beds](/uploads/BestCatBedsforIndoorCats5.webp)

| Material | Potential Benefits | Things to Consider |
|---|---|---|
| Plush fabric | Soft and cozy texture | Can collect loose fur |
| Fleece | Comfortable and soft | Check washing instructions |
| Cotton | Breathable feel | Quality depends on construction |
| Polyester | Common in cushions and covers | Quality varies |
| Memory foam | Can provide cushioning and support | Check thickness and construction |

Choose materials that are comfortable, properly finished, and suitable for regular use.

Avoid beds with strong chemical smells, exposed sharp components, loose decorative pieces, or damaged stitching.

### 3. Get the Right Size

Size is one of the most important factors when buying a cat bed.

A bed might look spacious in a product photograph but feel surprisingly small once your cat settles inside.

![Cat bed size guide showing sleeping length, width, and usable bed dimensions](/uploads/BestCatBedsforIndoorCats4.webp)

Measure your cat while they're sleeping in their usual position. Consider how much room they need to turn around, curl up, or stretch out.

Also, remember that manufacturers often display the outside dimensions rather than the actual usable sleeping area.

If you're choosing a bed for a growing kitten, consider how quickly they'll outgrow smaller designs.

### 4. Check Cushioning and Support

A comfortable sleeping surface should provide enough cushioning without becoming excessively soft or unstable.

Pay attention to:

- Cushion thickness.
- Filling quality.
- Whether the cushion is removable.
- Whether the bed retains its shape.
- Whether the sleeping surface feels suitable for your cat.

For senior cats or cats with mobility concerns, a supportive, low-entry design may be worth considering.

Orthopedic-style cat beds may also be useful, but the suitability of a particular product depends on its construction and your cat's needs.

### 5. Look for Easy Cleaning and Maintenance

Cat beds collect loose fur, dust, and everyday dirt.

A bed that's difficult to clean can quickly become unpleasant, even if it initially looks beautiful.

Look for:

- Removable and washable covers.
- Machine-washable materials where permitted.
- Cushion inserts that can be cleaned separately.
- Fabrics that are easy to remove loose fur from.
- Clear washing and drying instructions.

Always follow the manufacturer's care instructions to prevent shrinking or damage.

### 6. Consider Your Home's Temperature

Indoor cats may prefer different sleeping surfaces depending on the weather.

During colder months, a soft, cozy bed can provide a comfortable resting place. During warmer weather, some cats may prefer a more breathable and open surface.

If your home gets particularly warm, avoid choosing an excessively thick or insulating bed simply because it looks comfortable.

Providing different resting locations allows your cat to choose where they feel most comfortable.

### 7. Check Stability and Construction

A cat bed should stay in place during normal use.

Before purchasing, check:

- Secure stitching.
- Well-constructed seams.
- A stable base.
- Properly finished fabric edges.
- Cushioning that doesn't easily flatten.
- No exposed sharp or potentially hazardous components.

For elevated or window-mounted beds, stability is especially important. Follow installation instructions and verify the weight capacity before allowing your cat to use them.

## 6 Popular Types of Cat Beds and Their Benefits

Not all cat beds are designed in the same way. Understanding the different styles can help you narrow down your options.

![Comparison infographic showing six popular types of cat beds including donut, cave, bolster, and cushion designs](/uploads/BestCatBedsforIndoorCats2.webp)

### 1. Round Cat Beds

Round cat beds are designed to provide a defined sleeping area.

They can be suitable for cats that enjoy curling up and sleeping in a compact position.

### 2. Donut Cat Beds

Donut cat beds usually feature a soft sleeping surface surrounded by raised, cushioned edges.

Some cats enjoy resting their heads against these edges while curling up.

When buying one, check the actual sleeping area because thick raised sides can reduce the usable space.

### 3. Cave Cat Beds

Cave-style beds provide a more enclosed sleeping environment.

They may appeal to cats that enjoy hiding under blankets, resting in boxes, or sleeping in quiet corners.

However, some cats dislike enclosed spaces, so observe your cat's behavior before choosing this design.

### 4. Flat Cushion Beds

Flat cushion beds provide an open sleeping surface without high surrounding edges.

They can be a practical choice for cats that prefer stretching out or changing sleeping positions frequently.

They are also relatively easy to place on furniture or in quiet corners.

### 5. Bolster Cat Beds

Bolster beds have raised edges around some or all of the sleeping area.

These edges can provide a convenient surface for cats that enjoy resting their heads against something.

Check the height of the edges and make sure your cat can enter and exit comfortably.

### 6. Orthopedic Cat Beds

Orthopedic-style beds are designed with cushioning and support in mind.

Some use foam-based construction to distribute pressure across the sleeping surface.

They may be worth considering for senior cats or cats with mobility issues. However, don't rely on marketing claims alone. Check the materials and consult your veterinarian if your cat has specific support needs.

## Where to Put a Cat Bed in Your Home

Even a comfortable cat bed may remain unused if you place it in an unsuitable location.

Cats often prefer resting somewhere familiar, quiet, and safe.

![Indoor cat resting in a cozy bed beside a sunny window in a modern home](/uploads/BestCatBedsforIndoorCats6.webp)

Here are some places worth considering:

- **A quiet corner:** Choose an area away from heavy foot traffic.
- **Near a window:** Many cats enjoy watching birds and observing outdoor activity. Ensure the window is safely screened.
- **In a familiar room:** Try placing the bed where your cat already spends time.
- **Near a favorite sleeping spot:** This can make the new bed feel less unfamiliar.
- **Away from loud appliances:** Washing machines, noisy fans, and other disturbances may interrupt resting.

If your cat already has a favorite sleeping location, try placing the new bed nearby.

Don't force your cat to use it. Let them investigate and settle in naturally.

## Why Does My Cat Refuse to Sleep in Its New Bed?

You've purchased a beautiful new bed, carefully placed it in a comfortable corner, and your cat chooses the laundry basket instead.

Sound familiar?

![Orange cat sitting inside a cardboard box beside an unused new cat bed](/uploads/BestCatBedsforIndoorCats7.webp)

There are several possible reasons.

### 1. Your Cat Prefers Familiar Smells

Cats rely heavily on scent.

A new bed may smell unfamiliar, while your blanket or clothes carry familiar household scents.

Try placing a clean blanket that your cat already uses inside the new bed.

### 2. The Bed Doesn't Match Their Sleeping Style

A cat that loves stretching out may dislike an enclosed bed.

Another cat might find an open cushion too exposed.

This is why observing your cat's sleeping habits before purchasing is so important.

### 3. The Location Isn't Comfortable

Your cat may prefer a quieter, warmer, or more elevated location.

Try moving the bed to an area where your cat already enjoys resting.

### 4. The Material Feels Unfamiliar

Some cats are particular about textures.

They may avoid certain fabrics even when the bed looks comfortable.

Give your cat time to investigate the new sleeping surface.

### 5. Your Cat Simply Has Another Favorite Spot

Sometimes the sofa, cardboard box, or sunny patch on the floor is simply more appealing.

Cats can be wonderfully unpredictable!

The important thing is to give your cat choices rather than forcing them to use a particular bed.

## 5 Cat Bed Buying Mistakes to Avoid

Before placing your order, keep these common mistakes in mind.

### 1. Choosing Based Only on Appearance

A beautiful bed isn't necessarily a comfortable one.

Focus on the sleeping surface, construction, and your cat's preferences.

### 2. Ignoring Measurements

Always compare the product dimensions with your cat's size.

Don't rely entirely on product photographs.

### 3. Overlooking Cleaning Requirements

Loose fur, dust, and dirt can accumulate quickly.

Check whether the cover is removable and how the bed should be washed.

### 4. Buying an Unsuitable Design

Consider whether your cat curls up, stretches out, or prefers hiding.

The right design depends on your cat's habits.

### 5. Expecting Immediate Acceptance

Your cat may not use a new bed immediately.

Give them time to become familiar with its scent, texture, and location.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Cat Beds

### What type of bed do indoor cats like best?

It depends on the individual cat. Cats that curl up may enjoy round or donut-shaped beds, while those that stretch out may prefer flat cushions. Cats that enjoy hiding might feel more comfortable in enclosed beds.

### Do indoor cats really need a cat bed?

A dedicated cat bed isn't essential if your cat already has comfortable and safe resting places. However, a suitable bed can provide a designated sleeping area and help keep loose fur away from your furniture.

### Should I buy a covered or open cat bed?

Choose based on your cat's behavior. Cats that enjoy hiding may appreciate covered beds, while cats that prefer watching their surroundings may prefer open designs.

### Are washable cat beds better than non-washable beds?

Washable cat beds are generally more convenient to maintain, particularly in homes with heavy-shedding cats. Look for removable covers or clearly stated washing instructions before purchasing.

### Should I get a separate bed for each indoor cat?

Ideally, provide multiple comfortable resting spaces in a multi-cat household. Cats may prefer different sleeping locations, and having alternatives can reduce competition over favorite spots.

### How do I get my cat to use a new cat bed?

Place the bed somewhere your cat already enjoys resting. You can also add a familiar blanket or toy and allow your cat to investigate it naturally.

### Are orthopedic cat beds worth buying?

They may be worth considering for cats that need additional cushioning or have mobility concerns. However, product quality and suitability vary, so discuss specific support needs with your veterinarian.
