## Which Cat Tree Should You Choose?

Not every cat needs the same type of climbing furniture. Your apartment layout and your cat's personality should influence your decision.

Here's a quick guide to help you identify what to prioritize.

| Your needs | What to prioritize |
|---|---|
| Very small apartment | Compact footprint and vertical design |
| Active kitten | Multiple climbing levels and accessible platforms |
| Large adult cat | Wide platforms and a sturdy base |
| Frequent scratcher | Durable sisal scratching surfaces |
| Senior cat | Low platforms and easy access |
| Multi-cat household | Multiple resting areas and sufficient space |

**Our buying tip:** Don't automatically choose the tallest or largest cat tree. Look for a design that provides useful climbing and resting opportunities while fitting comfortably into your available space.

## How to Choose the Right Cat Tree for a Small Apartment

A cat tree should provide your feline friend with enough opportunities to climb, scratch, and rest without making your apartment feel cramped.

Here are six things to consider before purchasing.

### 1. Measure Your Available Floor Space

In a small apartment, every square foot matters.

Before ordering, measure the area where you plan to place the cat tree. Pay attention to its base width, depth, and the surrounding space needed for your cat to jump on and off comfortably.

![Diagram showing how to measure cat tree height, base width, depth, and available apartment floor space](/uploads/bestcattreesforsmallapartments4.webp)

Good placement options include:

- An unused corner of your living room.
- A suitable space beside the sofa.
- A quiet bedroom corner.
- An area near a securely protected window.

Avoid placing the cat tree where it blocks walkways, doors, or other frequently used spaces.

### 2. Prioritize Vertical Climbing Space

When floor space is limited, vertical climbing opportunities become particularly useful.

A thoughtfully designed multi-level cat tree can provide elevated resting areas and climbing platforms without requiring a particularly large footprint.

Look for:

- Multiple accessible platforms.
- Comfortable elevated resting areas.
- Suitable distances between climbing levels.
- A design that allows easy movement between platforms.

However, taller doesn't automatically mean better. Always prioritize stability and accessibility.

### 3. Check the Base and Overall Stability

Cats don't always climb gently. Some launch themselves onto platforms with surprising enthusiasm!

A poorly balanced cat tree can wobble or tip, potentially frightening or injuring your pet.

![Cat tree safety diagram highlighting a sturdy base, supporting posts, secure platforms, and wall anchoring](/uploads/bestcattreesforsmallapartments5.webp)

Before purchasing, check for:

- A sufficiently wide and sturdy base.
- Strong connecting posts.
- Securely attached platforms.
- Clear weight-capacity information.
- Wall-anchoring hardware when recommended by the manufacturer.

If you have an especially active or larger cat, stability deserves extra attention.

### 4. Choose Durable Scratching Materials

Scratching is a natural feline behavior. It helps cats maintain their claws, stretch their bodies, and mark their territory.

Sisal rope and sisal fabric are popular materials for cat scratching surfaces because they provide a textured surface that cats can grip.

![Close-up of natural sisal rope scratching material on a cat tree](/uploads/bestcattreesforsmallapartments6.webp)

When comparing products, look for:

- Substantial scratching areas.
- Securely attached scratching material.
- Suitable scratching-post height.
- Replaceable scratching components where available.

A cat tree with integrated scratching areas can provide more functionality without requiring separate scratching furniture.

### 5. Consider Your Cat's Size and Personality

A small kitten and a large adult cat don't necessarily need the same furniture.

Think about your cat's behavior before choosing a model.

- **Active climbers:** Look for multiple platforms and accessible climbing routes.
- **Relaxed sleepers:** Prioritize comfortable resting areas.
- **Frequent scratchers:** Choose models with substantial scratching surfaces.
- **Larger cats:** Pay attention to platform dimensions and weight capacity.
- **Senior cats:** Look for lower platforms and easier access.

Your cat's personality matters just as much as the furniture's appearance.

### 6. Check Assembly and Cleaning Requirements

A cat tree should make your life easier, not create another maintenance headache.

Before buying, check whether the product has clear assembly instructions, removable cushions, washable covers, or replaceable scratching components.

Regularly vacuum loose fur and inspect connecting screws, platforms, and scratching surfaces to keep the furniture in good condition.

## 5 Cat Tree Buying Mistakes Apartment Owners Should Avoid

Even an attractive cat tree can turn out to be a poor purchase if it doesn't suit your home.

Watch out for these common mistakes.

### 1. Choosing Appearance Over Stability

A beautiful cat tree isn't necessarily a safe one. Prioritize sturdy construction, secure connections, and a stable base over decorative features.

### 2. Ignoring Your Cat's Weight

Some platforms are designed for smaller cats and may not comfortably accommodate larger breeds. Always check the manufacturer's recommended weight capacity.

### 3. Buying Something Too Large

A massive cat tower can make an already small room feel cramped. Measure your available space and consider how the furniture will affect everyday movement.

### 4. Forgetting About Scratching Needs

A cat tree without suitable scratching surfaces may not satisfy your cat's natural scratching instincts. Look for a model that provides dedicated scratching areas.

### 5. Ignoring Placement and Accessibility

A cat tree placed in an isolated or inconvenient location may not attract much attention. Choose a suitable area where your cat can comfortably explore and observe its surroundings.

## Do Indoor Cats Really Need a Cat Tree?

While a cat tree isn't essential furniture for every household, it can make an indoor cat's environment considerably more interesting.

Cats naturally enjoy elevated resting places, climbing opportunities, and scratching surfaces. A suitable cat tree provides a dedicated space for these activities while helping redirect some scratching and climbing behavior away from household furniture.

For apartment-dwelling cats, vertical furniture can also make better use of limited living space.

The goal isn't to buy the biggest cat tree available. It's to provide an environment where your cat can comfortably express its natural behaviors.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Cat Trees for Small Apartments

### What is the best type of cat tree for a small apartment?

A compact cat tree with a stable base, multiple vertical platforms, and integrated scratching areas is generally a practical choice. Look for a design that provides useful climbing space without occupying too much floor area.

### How tall should a cat tree be for an apartment?

There isn't one ideal height for every apartment. Consider your available ceiling height, floor footprint, cat's climbing ability, and the stability of the structure before choosing a model.

### Are tall cat trees safe for cats?

Tall cat trees can be safe when properly designed, assembled, and positioned. Look for a stable base, secure connections, appropriate weight capacity, and wall anchoring when recommended by the manufacturer.

### Do cats actually use cat trees?

Many cats enjoy elevated resting areas, climbing opportunities, and scratching surfaces. However, individual preferences vary. Placing the tree in an interesting location and introducing it gradually can encourage use.

### What material is best for cat tree scratching posts?

Sisal rope and sisal fabric are popular choices because they provide textured surfaces for scratching. Check the construction quality and whether the scratching material can be replaced.

### Where should I place a cat tree in a small apartment?

A quiet corner near a secure window or in a room where your cat spends time can be a good starting point. Avoid blocking walkways, doors, or access to essential household areas.

### Can two cats share one cat tree?

Yes, provided the tree has enough appropriately sized platforms and resting areas. In multi-cat households, additional climbing and resting furniture can help reduce competition for space.

### How do I keep a cat tree clean?

Vacuum loose fur regularly, clean removable cushions according to their care instructions, and inspect scratching surfaces and connecting hardware. Replace worn or damaged components when necessary.

### Should I anchor a cat tree to the wall?

Some tall or narrow cat trees include wall-anchoring hardware or recommend securing the structure to a wall. Follow the manufacturer's installation instructions, particularly if your cat is active or the furniture is tall.

### How much floor space does a cat tree need?

It depends on the model. Check the manufacturer's listed width and depth, then account for enough surrounding clearance for your cat to climb and jump safely.
